\subsection{Strict vs.\ Same-Snapshot Amortization}
\label{sec:amortization_results}

Table~\ref{tab:amortization} compares the two evaluation tracks for the primary method pair.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{llrrrr}
\toprule
Track & Method & Mean F1 & Runtime & Setup & Total Cost \\
\midrule
Strict & GM (progressive) & 0.467 & 43,283 & 107,557 & 150,840 \\
Strict & RAG (progressive) & 0.286 & 15,587 & 414,082 & 429,669 \\
Same-snapshot & GM (progressive) & 0.544 & 42,136 & 9,018 & 51,154 \\
Same-snapshot & RAG (progressive) & 0.259 & 15,345 & 36,736 & 52,081 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Track-separated repeat means (\texttt{psf/requests}, $n=10$). In same-snapshot mode, setup costs collapse due to single index build.}
\label{tab:amortization}
\end{table}

Key observations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item In the strict track, \texttt{commit\_repeat\_ratio = 0.0} and \texttt{cache\_hit\_rate = 0.0}: every issue requires a fresh index build.
  \item In the same-snapshot track, \texttt{commit\_repeat\_ratio = 0.9} and \texttt{cache\_hit\_rate = 0.9}: the graph is built once and reused.
  \item GM-progressive F1 improves from 0.467 (strict) to 0.544 (same-snapshot), suggesting that index stability benefits graph navigation.
  \item The same-snapshot GM-progressive minus RAG-progressive delta is $+0.286$ with 95\% CI $[0.219, 0.333]$.
\end{itemize}

\caption{Token cost across 3 SWE-bench repos (Flask, Requests, Pytest; 30 issues total).}
\label{tab:cost_cross_repo}
\end{table}

GM methods achieve $\sim$3.2$\times$ lower setup cost than RAG methods (701K vs.\ 2.27M tokens) due to embedding compact graph metadata rather than full code bodies. GM-progressive total cost is 0.42$\times$ that of RAG-progressive despite 2.4$\times$ higher runtime token usage, because the setup cost dominates the total.
